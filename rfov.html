Hi Becca (and team),

I'm excited to apply for the Development Coordinator position with Roaring Fork Outdoor Volunteers.

Throughout my career, I've helped organizations build reliable data systems, communicate with people in meaningful ways, and create processes that keep the behind-the-scenes running smoothly. Outside of work, I've spent just as many years building trails, teaching outdoor education, coaching mountain biking, and volunteering in the communities that care for our public lands. RFOV is one of the few organizations where those two parts of my experience naturally come together.

I've spent more than fifteen years building the kinds of systems development teams rely on: CRM databases, customer segmentation, reporting dashboards, email campaigns, and clean, reliable data that supports meaningful relationships. I enjoy bringing order to complexity—organizing messy information, improving data quality, building reports that help guide decisions, and creating processes that people trust. That includes hands-on Salesforce work — Einstein and Marketing Cloud — at Zayo. Most recently, I partnered with Sunlight Ski & Bike in Glenwood Springs to clean and standardize thousands of inconsistent customer and product records, improving reporting and creating a stronger foundation for future marketing and business decisions.

Communication has always been just as important as the data itself. I've written email campaigns, newsletters, landing pages, and marketing communications that reached thousands of recipients while improving engagement and inspiring action. I enjoy translating information into stories that help people feel connected to a mission, whether that's encouraging a customer to support a local business or inviting someone to become part of something bigger than themselves.

My connection to stewardship extends well beyond my professional experience. I participated in one of HMI's early summer gap trips, in the San Juans, taught outdoor education courses at Colgate University, spent summers working on trail crews in the White Mountains, and now serve as a PMBIA-certified mountain bike coach. Some of my favorite days are still the ones spent maintaining trails alongside volunteers or helping someone discover the confidence to explore the outdoors.

What resonates most about RFOV is your belief that conservation happens through community. You don't simply ask people to support public lands—you invite them to care for them. Volunteers swinging tools on a trail, students learning through stewardship, donors seeing the direct impact of their support—those are the kinds of connections that create lasting conservation. I would be proud to help build and strengthen those relationships through thoughtful communication, organized systems, and reliable data.

I enjoy making complicated things feel simple, whether that's organizing a database, planning an event, writing a fundraising email, or building dashboards that help teams make better decisions. Just as importantly, I enjoy helping people feel connected to a cause they can feel good about. The opportunity to combine those skills in support of RFOV's mission is exactly the kind of work I've been hoping to find.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I'd love the opportunity to discuss how my background in CRM management, communications, analytics, and community engagement could help support RFOV's fundraising and stewardship efforts.

04 Why RFOV specifically

This isn't a generic application.

Three reasons, in order of weight.

First, the model. A lot of conservation nonprofits ask you to fund the work from a distance. RFOV mobilizes thousands of volunteers to actually do it — 60 to 80 project sites a year, across sustainable recreation, healthy landscapes, and fire adaptation. That's the version of stewardship I've always found most honest, and it's the one I've personally practiced, not just funded.

Second, the skill combination. This role explicitly wants someone who's organized and data-driven and comfortable leading or attending a volunteer stewardship project. Most candidates will have one half of that. I have both, and neither one is borrowed for the application — the data half is fifteen years of my career, and the trail half is a genuinely current part of my life.

Third, the region. Serving Aspen to Parachute across Pitkin, Garfield, Eagle, and Gunnison counties is exactly the landscape I grew up around. I'm currently based in Boulder and would relocate to the Roaring Fork Valley for a role like this one — this isn't a remote application to a job I'd commute to occasionally.
